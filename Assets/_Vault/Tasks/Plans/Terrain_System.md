# Terrain System — authored, painted, streamed open world

One document for the whole thing: the architecture, every decision with its reason, a spike list that must run before the risky parts are built, and a task list sized for Sonnet workers.

**Goal.** Author an open world in the Editor the way you would paint it: paint ground layers, sculpt height, drop spline roads and rivers that shape the ground themselves, scatter decals and props from rule sets, and generate wilderness procedurally while hand-authoring hero spots. The world streams in 100 x 100 m tiles. The player drives a caravan across it, zooms out to a miniature strategic view at faster time, and zooms back in for events, while units, AI, A* and trade caravans keep working across tile borders.

## 1. The architecture in one page

**Your guess was right, with one refinement.** Units live outside the terrain scenes. The refinement: it is not "one scene vs another", it is *where entities live*.

| Thing | Lives in | Streamed? |
|---|---|---|
| Player, minions, enemies, spawned units | Runtime entities in the default world (no subscene) | Never. They survive any tile unloading |
| Managers, libraries, rules, UI, camera, `NavGridSettings` | Persistent scene + `CampLoop_Sub` (the subscene that exists today) | Never |
| Tile registry (`TerrainWorldAuthoring`) | Persistent subscene | Never |
| Ground mesh, ground collider, nav cost blob, scatter props, decals, spawn-point markers | **One SubScene per tile** (`Tile_x_z.unity`) | Yes, by distance to the player |
| Spline roads | Persistent scene (`TerrainRoad` objects) | Authoring only; they bake *into* the tiles they cross |

**Rule that follows from it:** a tile scene holds only static world and *markers*. Anything that must survive the tile unloading (people, animals, loot) is spawned at runtime from a marker, never baked into the tile.

### World simulation LOD: units keep existing when their tile is unloaded

Freezing units on unloaded tiles was rejected (owner call, 2026-10-30): walk away, come back, and the same unit standing in the same spot reads as "inactive", and later the player schedules trade routes, so caravans must genuinely be *travelling* the world while the player is elsewhere. The design is a cheap second tier of simulation, not a pause.

| Tier | When | Cost per unit | What runs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Active** | its tile is loaded and it is within the materialize radius of the player | full | AI, A*, physics, animation, everything |
| **Travelling** (abstract) | not active, has a route | about zero per frame | nothing. Its position is a **pure function of world time**: `PointAlongRoute(route, (now - departure) * speed)`, evaluated only when needed |
| **Resting** (abstract) | not active, no route | about zero | stays at its anchor, or at its schedule slot for the current time of day |

- **Same entity, never despawned.** Going abstract enables `AbstractSimulation` on the unit and gates movement, gravity, AI and rendering off. Health, cargo, relationships and identity survive with no serialisation and no respawn.
- **Leaving.** When a unit's tile is about to unload (or it falls outside the keep radius) `AbstractTransitionSystem` turns its current intent into a route: a goal becomes a straight leg or a road-graph path to it; a schedule becomes its next slot; nothing becomes a rest anchor. It stamps `departureWorldSeconds`.
- **Returning.** When the tile is `Loaded`, the collider exists, and the unit is within the materialize radius: compute `PointAlongRoute` at the current world time, raycast the ground height, re-enable the unit, and give it a real `PathRequest` to the next waypoint so it carries on walking the same road. Because the load radius is larger than what the camera can see, units appear walking in from beyond the edge, not popping in.
- **Time.** Driven by a monotonic `totalWorldSeconds` on `WorldClock` (task P9-0), so it respects pause and a sleep/day-skip (a caravan can finish its route during a skip). Analytic position means there is no per-frame stepping at all and ten thousand travellers cost nothing until they approach.
- **Trade integration.** Scheduled caravans are *born abstract*: the trade system calls `AbstractTravel.BeginRoute(entity, waypoints, speed)` and the unit only becomes active when it passes near the player. This is what keeps background trade routes light.
- **Roads are the route network.** Spline roads bake a `RoadGraph` blob into the persistent subscene (nodes at endpoints and junctions, edges with sampled polylines and lengths). A route is a tiny search over a graph of tens of nodes and works with every tile unloaded. Off-road legs are straight segments in v1.
- **Encounters.** Everything inside the loaded ring is active, so two groups that meet near the player are both real and fight, trade or ignore each other through the normal AI. Two abstract groups that cross paths far from the player simply pass (safe travel, owner call). **Not in v1:** combat or death between abstract groups, needs/AI progress while abstract (the schedule slot is the fake); `AbstractEncounterEvent` is reserved for that later.
- **Pinned units.** Companions and squad members carry `PinnedActive` and never go abstract; they follow the player into new tiles anyway.

**Heights, collisions, nav**
- Terrain is our own mesh per tile, **not** Unity's built-in Terrain (it does not render through Entities Graphics and our painted shader is world-projected anyway).
- Collision is the Unity Physics `TerrainCollider` heightfield if the spike confirms it works with our unit gravity, otherwise a baked `MeshCollider`.
- Nav stays **one global grid** (the toolkit's design) sized to the whole world bounds. Each tile bakes its own 100 x 100 cost map offline; at runtime a stitch system blits a tile's costs into the grid when the tile loads and fills that region with "wall" when it unloads. That kills the current "rebuild the entire grid with overlap queries on every physics body-count change" behaviour, which streaming would trigger constantly.

**Authoring.** One window, `Stitch Punk > Terrain > Terrain Editor`, with a tab per job and a live Scene-view brush. Everything you author is either a **ScriptableObject set** (palette, decal set, scatter set, road profile — reusable) or **data on the tile** (heightmap, mask, instances).

**Non-destructive layering.** Hand-painted mask and height are *source*. Roads are *source*. The tile bakes a **composite** (paint + roads) into the SDF the shader reads. Move a road and only the tiles it crosses re-composite; your hand painting is never overwritten.

### Scales of representation: what exists at each camera zoom

| Zoom | Detail tiles | Strategic layer | Props and decals | Simulation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Close** (normal caravan camera) | 3 x 3 ring around the focus | hidden | full | active units; caravan and pinned units full; others abstract |
| **Mid** | same ring | fades in beyond the ring | culled by distance, faded | same |
| **Strategic** (miniature) | ring stays loaded so the caravan has ground to drive on | the whole map | off | world time at 2-4x; only the caravan and pinned units at full detail; everything else abstract (analytic, free) |

- **The strategic layer** is baked from tile data: one coarse terrain mesh per 10 x 10-tile super-chunk (4 m grid) plus one colour texture per chunk painted from the composite masks, the palette's strategic colours, roads, rivers and water, with hill shading baked in. About nine chunks for a 3 km world, always resident, tens of MB at most. It is not a second map to author: it is regenerated from the tiles.
- **The seam** between detail tiles and the strategic mesh is handled with a small depth bias plus a distance fade on the strategic material, so the detail ring simply draws over it.
- **The miniature look is a camera blend.** The existing caravan (Vehicle) camera is the zero. A new **Strategic camera** (a second `CinemachineCamera`) defines the far view: steeper pitch (about 40 degrees), narrow FOV, long distance, following the caravan. A **`CinemachineMixingCamera`** named `CaravanZoomMix` holds both as children, and a **toggle key** animates the Strategic camera's weight from 0 to 1 (`mix01`) over about 1.5 seconds with an ease curve, and back again; there is no scroll zoom while driving the caravan. Because the cameras themselves carry pitch, FOV and distance, those blend by Cinemachine with no curves of ours. Everything else is keyed to `mix01` through `StrategicZoomProfile` (TT-D31): a Cinemachine Volume Settings extension on the Strategic camera brings in the warm desaturated grade and vignette as it blends, **depth-of-field focused on the caravan only in the super far range** (curve exactly 0 until `mix01` of about 0.75, owner call), stronger directional light and shadows on the ground, the visual load radius, and faster time. Built from built-in URP Volume overrides, **not** the custom painted post pass (it rendered black on this Unity version). Recorded exception: the gameplay camera has a fixed angle by design (see the comment in `PlayerInputManager`); the Strategic camera adds a second fixed angle and the blend changes pitch, yaw stays fixed.
- **Detail reach at far zoom.** In the reference, everything inside roughly 600 m is visible. The sharp band is within the 3 x 3 ring, but the soft areas beyond need real-looking forests and fields too, so the visual load radius grows with zoom (TT-D32) and the strategic layer carries canopy texture and field patches rather than flat colour.

### Terrain layering (non-destructive)

Every source is stored separately per tile; the compositor rebuilds the final data from them.

```
final height = procedural base + hand-sculpt delta + river carve + road carve
final mask   = procedural base mask
               overridden by hand paint overlay (where painted)
               overridden by river banks / shallows
               overridden by roads
```

`TerrainRegion` volumes in the scene choose `Hand`, `Procedural` or `Mixed` for the tiles inside them. `Hand` tiles are never touched by the generator, `Procedural` tiles can be regenerated freely, `Mixed` regenerates the base and keeps your overlays. Moving a road or river re-composites only the tiles it crosses.

## 2. Decisions (TT-D6 onward; D1-D5 are in TerrainTiles_System.md)

Defaults are chosen so the build can run unattended. Change one and the affected tasks name which TT-D they depend on.

- **TT-D6 Tile = scene.** `Assets/Terrain/Tiles/Tile_{x}_{z}/Tile_{x}_{z}.unity` plus its data assets in the same folder. Tile (x,z) is centred on (x*100, z*100). *Why:* a scene per tile is the only unit Entities can stream, and one folder per tile keeps assets findable.
- **TT-D7 Heightmap resolution 101 x 101 samples (1 m), float, stored in a `TerrainTileData` ScriptableObject.** Edge rows are shared with the neighbour (sample 100 of tile x == sample 0 of tile x+1) and edits near an edge write both. *Why:* matches the nav cell size, no cracks, 10k vertices per tile.
- **TT-D8 (revised) Splat mask is 256 px per tile by default (about 0.4 m per texel) and 512 px for hero tiles, set per tile; still 3 turf channels + base dirt.** SDF range stays 4 m. *Why:* 900 tiles at 512 px would be about 1 GB of masks; wilderness does not need that resolution, and the Steam Deck budget (TT-D28) cannot afford it. A seven-layer shader extension stays out of v1.
- **TT-D9 Roads = splat surface + decals along the spline.** The spline rasterises a width/falloff band into a per-tile road mask that composites over the paint mask as the profile's layer (dirt/stone/grass), optionally flattens height with shoulders, and then the existing decal pipeline places ruts, edge stones, puddles along it. A draped ribbon mesh is an optional later phase (needs a new shader). *Why:* reuses everything built, inherits the painted fringe edges for free, and a spline edit is a cheap re-bake.
- **TT-D10 Tile contents are GameObjects in the tile scene** (decals under `Decals`, props under `Scatter`), tagged by marker components so the tools can regenerate, clear, and *lock* hand-placed ones. *Why:* you can see and hand-edit them with normal Editor tools, and they bake through the normal pipeline. Roughly 500-2000 props per tile is fine.
- **TT-D11 Decals bake to entities** if spike S3 passes (`PAINTED_ENTITIES` + per-instance tile/tint). If it fails, fallback is baking a per-tile decal texture sampled by the ground shader (no entities, no depth-texture dependency).
- **TT-D12 (revised) Nav is a sliding window over the loaded ring, not a world grid.** The grid covers (2R+3) x (2R+3) tiles around the focus (5 x 5 = 500 x 500 cells at 1 m for R = 1, 250k cells per layer, about 750k with three layers for interiors). When the focus enters a new tile, or a tile loads or unloads, `NavWindowSystem` re-centres the window by whole tiles and rewrites the cost map from the loaded tiles' in-memory blobs (25 x 10k byte copies, trivial), updates `NavGridConfig.gridOrigin` and bumps `costMapVersion` so cached paths and flow fields invalidate. *Why:* a 3 km world at 1 m cells is 9M cells per layer and `FlowFieldSystem` allocates a `cellsPerLayer` queue per field build (36 MB); a window keeps every cost small and constant. The only package change is letting the origin move at runtime (P8-1).
- **TT-D13 Two-tier simulation: full sim only in loaded tiles, abstract sim everywhere else (replaces the earlier "freeze units" idea; owner call 2026-10-30).** A unit on an unloaded tile would fall through the world, but freezing it makes the world feel dead and breaks the later trade-route scheduling. So units outside the active region switch to `AbstractSimulation` (see the World simulation LOD section): route-following as a pure function of world time, or a schedule slot. This is a recorded deviation from the `CameraVisible` rule in RULES.md (that rule is about presentation; this is about world existence). Add a Gotchas entry when built.
- **TT-D14 Streaming focus = the player entity** (camera target as fallback). `simLoadRadiusTiles` = 1 (3 x 3: physics, nav window, materialization), unload radius one tile beyond, max 1 tile load started per frame. All are fields on `TerrainWorldProfile`. See TT-D32 for the larger visual radius at far zoom.
- **TT-D15 No Unity Terrain, no Polybrush, no third-party packages.** Unity Splines (already in the project via Cinemachine, version 2.8.4) is the only new dependency and becomes a direct reference.
- **TT-D16 Spline roads also bake a `RoadGraph`** (nodes at endpoints and junctions within 2 m of each other, edges with sampled polylines and lengths) into a blob in the persistent subscene. *Why:* it is the route network for abstract travellers and, later, for trade-route scheduling and a player-facing map, and it makes cross-world routing independent of which tiles are loaded.
- **TT-D17 Abstract travel is analytic, not stepped.** A route stores departure world-seconds, speed and cumulative waypoint lengths; position is computed on demand. *Why:* zero per-frame cost, correct through pause and day skips, and trivially deterministic for save/load.
- **TT-D18 Resting units use a schedule slot, not a frozen pose.** If a unit has a schedule (the Schedules feature reads `WorldClock.timeOfDay01`), its abstract position is where that slot says it should be at the current time; materializing it there is what stops "same unit, same spot". Units with neither route nor schedule rest at an anchor.
- **TT-D19 Strategic layer** = baked coarse super-chunks, always loaded, regenerated from tile data (see Scales of representation). Strategic zoom keeps the detail ring loaded around the caravan so driving still has collision.
- **TT-D20 Time scale is a single `WorldTimeScale` singleton driven by camera zoom** (default curve 1x close to 3x far, as `timeScaleTarget` in `StrategicZoomProfile`). Abstract positions are functions of world seconds so they are unaffected by how time is scaled; pinned units use `SystemAPI.Time.DeltaTime` and scale automatically (verify physics stays stable at 3x in the spike).
- **TT-D21 Layer order** is procedural base, hand overlay, river, road, composite (see Terrain layering). Each source is a separate array/texture in `TerrainTileData`; nothing the generator writes ever overwrites hand data.
- **TT-D22 Water.** Rivers are splines that carve a bed and add a flowing water ribbon whose level never rises downstream; lakes and sea are fixed-level water bodies; the bank band is *shallows* (walkable, heavy nav cost, speed x0.5) and the deep centre (depth over 1.2 m) is a wall. Crossings are `TerrainCrossing` markers: a **Bridge** forces nav cells walkable under its deck, a **Ford** forces a river segment to shallow. No boats, no swimming.
- **TT-D23 Surface map.** Each tile's nav blob also stores one surface-type byte per cell (`Normal, Road, Shallow, Deep, Mud, Rock`). Speed multipliers per surface (road 1.25, shallow 0.5, others 1) live on `TerrainWorldProfile`; footsteps can read the same map later.
- **TT-D24 Player-placed structures** (and the caravan workstation) are runtime entities in the persistent world, never baked into a tile. They carry a `NavFootprint`; whenever a tile's costs are written into the window the footprints inside it are re-stamped. They save per tile in the existing Save group. Placement requires slope under a threshold and no overlap; there is no terrain deformation.
- **TT-D25 Population.** `TerrainSpawnMarker` in a tile scene: *persistent* markers (camps, towns) spawn once (a saved flag stops respawns) and the units then live on forever through the abstract tier; *on-demand* markers (wildlife, random enemies) spawn when the tile loads and despawn when it unloads. Traders and caravans are created by the trade system and are abstract from birth.
- **TT-D26 Per-tile `TerrainPalette`** supplies the snow and rock look for the mountain tiles and any regional look (the Grass slot can be snow). The seven-layer extension stays out of v1.
- **TT-D27 Multi-floor interiors are a separate spec** (`Interiors_Nav_System.md`, written after P8). This plan only guarantees that `layerCount` greater than 1 survives the sliding window, that building prefabs placed in tile scenes can contribute a nav patch for upper layers, and that no terrain task assumes a single layer.
- **TT-D28 Steam Deck budgets.** Nine loaded tiles at most 150 MB resident terrain data; at most 600 decals plus props drawn per tile after culling; at most 1.2 MB on disk per baked wilderness tile (256 px masks, compressed); strategic layer at most 40 MB. P10 measures these; a task that cannot meet them is a bug, not a trade-off.
- **TT-D29 Lighting.** The ground reads global shader values (`_TerrainAmbientColor`, `_TerrainLightningFlash`) set by one `TerrainLightingBridge` MonoBehaviour from the existing day/night driver. A lightning strike is a one-frame spike on the flash value. No per-tile data depends on time of day.
- **TT-D30 Build order is tools first** (owner call): P1 to P7 and P3c produce an authorable world before streaming, nav, the strategic view and abstract travel.
- **TT-D31 The strategic look is a camera blend plus one profile.** Rig: a `CinemachineMixingCamera` (`CaravanZoomMix`) with two child `CinemachineCamera`s: the existing caravan camera at weight `1 - mix01` (zero point, unchanged framing) and a new `StrategicCamera` at weight `mix01` (pitch, FOV, distance authored on the camera itself, matched to the reference image). `CameraManager`'s `Vehicle` slot points at the mixing camera so the existing priority switching, cutscene and cinematic entry/exit keep working untouched. `CameraZoomController` is **left alone** (it still zooms on foot; caravan mode has no scroll zoom, owner call). A new action `ToggleStrategicView` in the Vehicle input map flips a target between 0 and 1 and `mix01` eases to it over `transitionSeconds` (default 1.5), so the late blur and every other curve play out as a short cinematic transition rather than a scroll position. `StrategicZoomProfile` (curves over `mix01`) keys everything the cameras do not: depth-of-field strength (**exactly 0 until about 0.75, then easing to full at 1**), ground directional-light strength, strategic layer fade, visual load radius, scatter draw distance, HUD fade and the `WorldTimeScale` target. Grade and vignette ride a `CinemachineVolumeSettings` extension on the Strategic camera so they scale in with the blend (spike S13 confirms this works for a Mixing Camera child, else the bridge sets a Volume weight from `mix01`). Depth-of-field is **depth-based, focused at the caravan's distance** (URP Bokeh or Gaussian per spike S12) so the top and bottom blur naturally on the angled camera; the override is disabled while its curve is 0 so it costs nothing at close and mid zoom. If cost on the Steam Deck is too high the fallback is a screen-Y gradient blur through the built-in `FullScreenPassRendererFeature` with a minimal shader. The look reaches the reference at `mix01` = 1.
- **TT-D32 Visual load radius is separate from sim load radius.** At far zoom the view spans about 600 m, so `visualLoadRadiusTiles` ramps from 1 to 2 (5 x 5 tiles) with `mix01`; tiles beyond the sim radius load for looks only (their nav blobs are not blitted, units there stay abstract). Props and decals beyond a distance are thinned or swapped to a simple stand-in per `TerrainScatterSet` LOD fields. P11 measures this against the TT-D28 Steam Deck budgets and may cap the radius (`visualLoadRadiusTiles` is a curve over `mix01` in `StrategicZoomProfile`).
- **TT-D33 Named locations.** `TerrainLocationMarker` (name, type, radius, optional icon) in tile scenes bakes into a small registry in the persistent subscene, giving one source for the travel HUD ("heading to Blackwood Forest"), strategic map labels, road-graph node names and trade destinations.

### Open questions (defaults apply unless you say otherwise)
1. **World extents:** 30 x 30 tiles, tile (0,0) is the camp, so tiles run from -15 to +14 on both axes. 2. **Roads carve height by default:** yes, per-profile toggle. 3. **Draped ribbon road mesh:** not needed; splat plus decals is enough until you ask. 4. **Where does the mountain go and how tall?** Default: one mountain near a corner of the map, about 300 m tall, edited in `TerrainGenerationProfile`. 5. **How strong is the faster time?** Default curve 1x close to 3x strategic, tune by feel.

## 7. How to run this unattended

**Orchestrator = the main session.** Only the orchestrator touches the Unity MCP. Workers are `worker` (edits) or `verifier` (read-only), both Sonnet, per CLAUDE.md. Never `general-purpose`, never resume a capped agent: read its diff and spawn a fresh worker for the remainder.

**Standard footer for every worker brief** (paste verbatim):
> Read only the named line ranges. Match the surrounding code's style. No `var`, no single-letter names, explicit types, long descriptive names, no multi-paragraph comments (one or two lines of *why*). Never `.Run()` a job. Verify any Unity/Entities/Physics/Splines API against `Library/PackageCache` before calling it. Before writing your report run `python Tools/devloop/devloop.py gate <package-or-assembly>` and, for `Editor/` files, `python Tools/devloop/devloop.py lint <your paths>`. At turn 30 stop editing and report. Report in 30 lines or fewer: files changed, gate output, anything you could not verify.

**Per wave**
1. Orchestrator writes the briefs (snippets pasted in, exact names, line ranges), spawns the parallel group.
2. Read each worker's diff. If the gate result is exit 2 (`GATE STALE`) refresh Unity through MCP once, then re-gate; never treat 2 as a pass.
3. `refresh_unity` -> poll `isCompiling` -> `read_console` (errors only). `PaintedGround` and `Assets/` code without an asmdef compile only this way.
4. One commit per wave, stage paths explicitly, never `git add -A`; leave the owner's untracked files (`InitTestScene*`, `Screenshots`, trade prompt) alone.
5. Update `Terrain.md` and the memory file for anything non-obvious the wave taught.

**Standing traps (all already bitten this project)**
- A domain reload closes subscenes: reopen with `SubSceneUtility.EditScene` before touching tile objects; `manage_scene save` saves only the active scene, save a tile with `EditorSceneManager.SaveScene(scene)`.
- `execute_code` is CodeDom C# 6 and blocks `AssetDatabase.DeleteAsset` (use `manage_asset delete`).
- Burst static entry points take `in`/`ref`/`out`, not by-value structs; Burst log strings have format limits (see memory).
- Game view screenshots can be blank for a second after reopening a subscene: retry before debugging.
- The painted post-process effect rendered black on this Unity version; do not revisit it inside this plan.
- `Unity 6.5` has no `GetInstanceID`; use `GetEntityId()`.
- After a blob struct layout change, the first EditMode run can fail in untouched fixtures: recompile and re-run.

**Stop and report instead of continuing if:** a spike contradicts a TT-D that three or more later tasks depend on; a worker hits its turn cap twice on the same task (scope it smaller, do not retry the same brief); a change would need an edit outside the named files; the Editor will not respond for more than a few minutes; or a commit would include files you did not intend.

**Owner checkpoints.** Per the standing rule, unseen visual checkpoints close as accepted; only game-breaking problems escalate. The ones worth looking at when you are back are the P2, P5 and P9 checkpoints.
